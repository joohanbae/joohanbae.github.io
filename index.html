Research

I work on human–AI interaction, specifically on making the reasoning behind a model's output available for inspection and revision. My grounding is in argumentation theory and in Nishida Kitarō's concept of basho — the place from which a statement becomes sayable.

Three threads run through the work.

Extracting implicit premises

An LLM response is an enthymeme: an argument with its warrant left out. I develop methods to recover those warrants and represent them as objects a person can accept, reject, or rewrite.

Making the cost of vagueness visible

When an instruction is imprecise, someone pays for it — usually the person, in rework they did not anticipate. TokenTangram meters that cost visually at the moment of writing the command.

Argument as an interaction technique

A system where the person argues against the AI rather than the reverse. Built on Walton's argumentation schemes and Toulmin's warrant-gap framework, with the model's stance governed by explicit rules rather than free generation.

Background

Master's student in mechanical engineering at Kogakuin University, in the Misaki Laboratory, supervised by Daigo Misaki and Masa Ogawa. I came to HCI through building things: before graduate school I led the programming team of the Kogakuin Robot Project, writing control software for competition robots.

That route matters to how I work. I read philosophy for the questions and build systems for the answers, and I would rather ship a probe that fails informatively than argue from a diagram. I work in Korean, Japanese, and English.
